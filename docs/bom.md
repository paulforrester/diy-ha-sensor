**Decision for Paul: CO2 dominates the cost.**

- It is roughly **€530-600** of the run total.

  marketplace SCD40 boards, which carry a real counterfeit and refurbished risk.
- Apollo's own €23.99 SCD40 module doesn't fit this design. It plugs into a proprietary mezzanine connector on Apollo's
  board (Apollo docs, "Adding CO2 to MSR-2").

Options:

| Option | Cost of the CO2 line |
|---|---|
| (a) CO2 in all 10 units, genuine Adafruit SCD-40 (as tabled) | about €530-600 |
| (b) CO2 only in the rooms where ventilation matters, for example bedrooms and an office | about €50-55 per CO2 unit |
| (c) Unbranded SCD40 boards, checked on arrival (variant and serial commands, outdoor reading near 420 ppm) | about €10-25 each [S], with the risk above |

**I recommend (b).** Tell me which rooms, and I'll cut the quantity.

## 2. What changed since the proposal

- **Quantities:** a run of 10, not 4. Some units may go to the US: only the USB wall adapter differs (section 6).
- **CO2:** now wanted.
  - The proposal's cheapest genuine option, the Apollo module, is out (connector, above), so the Adafruit SCD-40 is
    the primary.
  - Adafruit's board has a 3.3 V regulator and level shifters, and runs from 3.3 to 5 V (Adafruit-SCD-4x-PCB README
    on GitHub).
  - Wiring plan: it runs from the XIAO's 3V3 by default. The I2C pull-ups follow the board's supply, so feeding it 5 V
    could put 5 V on the ESP32's I2C pins.
  - This is checked against Adafruit's schematic in Phase 2 [A].
- **Cellar probe:** one of the 10 units, plus genuine DS18B20+ chips built into probes (section 5). The separate
  cellar-probe project's parts are covered by this run.
- **USB supply and cable:** one each per unit (Paul's answer). A USB-A supply with an A-to-C cable avoids USB-C power
  negotiation questions entirely.

## 5. The cellar unit

The cellar unit is a standard unit plus DS18B20 probes, for bottle or air temperature at other points in the cellar.

- **Probes:**
  - Two probes are assumed (Paul to confirm), each built from a genuine DS18B20+ (TO-92) on a 2 m three-core cable.
  - Each is sealed with adhesive-lined heat shrink, optionally inside a 6 mm stainless sleeve.
  - Paul's soldering station and heat gun are enough.
- **Wiring:** 1-Wire on a free XIAO pin, proposed D2 / GPIO4, with a 4.7 kΩ pull-up to 3V3. ESPHome 2026.9.1 has the
  `one_wire` (`gpio` platform) and `dallas_temp` components. Firmware is Phase 2.
- **Acceptance check:** each probe's ROM code (printed in the ESPHome log) should match the genuine pattern
  `28-xx-xx-xx-xx-00-00-xx` reported by the same study.
- **Other sensors:** the radar and lux sensor stay. If this unit replaces the Aqara FP300 in the cellar, its presence
  and light take over from it there [A].
- **Humidity:** cellars run humid. The SHT45 handles 0-100 %RH [D]. Its heater stays off (proposal 4.4).
